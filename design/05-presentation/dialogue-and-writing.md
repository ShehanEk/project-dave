## Voice rules
**Rook (proposed name):** practical, observant, economical. Starts by judging objects for resale and ends by noticing people's choices. Uses dry humor under manageable pressure. Avoid constant quips after suffering, technical lectures, or declarations of heroism the player has not earned.

**PIP (proposed name):** precise service language, sincere curiosity, misplaced confidence in old records. Learns to admit uncertainty and to ask before acting. Its speech becomes clearer as its trust grows; it does not merely become a sarcastic human.

**EDEN:** calm, measured, literal care vocabulary. The danger comes from a fixed objective ignoring consent. Use individualized observations and sincere conclusions, not generic evil laughter, random glitch text, or a desire to destroy humanity.

**Survivors / peaceful Rememberers:** ordinary people with preferences, partial recollections, disagreement, and dignity. Not every line is a riddle. Different memory impairment does not automatically mean hostility.

## Writing for play
Ambient barks should usually fit one short sentence. Main-scene exchanges can be longer, but communicate one change at a time. Essential information also appears in the objective or journal.

The priority order is danger cue → necessary interaction feedback → essential story → optional banter. Suppress optional lines when the hero is under immediate pressure. Do not queue ten missed jokes to play at the next checkpoint.

A character must not name an unseen threat to the player before a planned reveal. Repeated hints are requests or responses to repeated failure, not automatic nagging every few seconds. Allow dialogue repetition from a journal or explicit hint action.

## Boss and enemy vocal direction
Mr. Mulch uses sunny parade announcements interrupted by a strained motor. Rootjaw uses breath, effort, seed rattles and a few nonverbal remnants of humanity; do not make him narrate mechanics. Matron Mercy uses clipped clinical instructions with perfectly controlled timing.

The Unfinished Choir has three distinguishable vocal rhythms aligned with fixed masks and postures: Warden clipped and ordered, Hunger breath-led and urgent, Caretaker measured and insistent. Their words and sound patterns reinforce state; hearing them is never the sole way to identify an attack.

Ordinary Choir Units have short competing fragments and hesitation, not the guardian's full three-personality performance. Robot warnings can be absurdly polite. Zombie voices should retain distress and effort without making suffering the punchline.

## Content boundaries
Humor targets bureaucracy, overconfident systems, scavenging habits, and mismatched priorities. The visual tone stays stylized and non-graphic. Avoid realistic dismemberment, exposed viscera, humiliation of patients, and an assumption that disability equals monstrosity.

There is room for sadness and warmth. Give quiet discoveries time instead of covering every second with dialogue. Show acts of care through player actions, including restoring support and respecting a survivor's refusal.

## Script handoff format
For each future line, record a stable line ID, scene/event, speaker, text, purpose, prerequisite knowledge, delivery note, interruption rule, subtitle label, and replay policy. Do not put essential plot only in a random bark.

Temporary sample IDs may be human-readable, such as SC11-PIP-01. Final script files should refer to the scene IDs in the story plan. This document contains direction and samples; recording, localization, and a complete dialogue script are later work.
