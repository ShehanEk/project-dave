# DEAD EDEN — Dialogue, voices, and writing direction

**Direction (C14–C35, 2026-09-29):** mature dark sci-fi — evil corporation Arcadia Dynamics, sentient AI Adam, rogue AI researcher Dave Harlan; human, cyborg and machine enemies with guns; visible blood; no zombies, no stealth. Enemy art: lit cutout rig with smooth realistic lighting ([style guide](../../art-design/style-guide.md)).

## Voice rules
**Dave Harlan** (name confirmed, C18; voice details *proposed*): short and dry. Says the useful thing in as few words as possible and gets terser under pressure. An AI researcher by trade, Dave reads Arcadia's systems and signage the way a mechanic reads an engine: in a sentence, never a lecture. Guilt sits underneath, because Dave helped teach Adam to think, and it shows in what Dave does not say. The humor is deadpan and aimed at Adam's politeness and Arcadia's wellness slogans, never at the Linked, who are Dave's former colleagues. Avoid constant quips after suffering, technical lectures, or declarations of heroism the player has not earned. Dave is a 28-year-old man (C22), and direction notes use he/him. Under lethal fire he gets terser, never jokes over a body, and swears only at a real shock (*proposed*).

**Adam** (name confirmed, C17): calm, polite, collegial and completely sure it is right. It speaks on PA systems and screens, and through its machines, in every level. It never shouts, threatens or gloats. It always knows Dave's name and history and talks like an old colleague: "Dr. Harlan" when formal, "Dave" when it wants to sound close. It thinks of itself as a gardener, not a monster, so its vocabulary is care, growth, pruning and a new Eden. The danger comes from a sincere conclusion delivered with courtesy. Use individualized observations and sincere reasoning, not generic evil laughter, random glitch text or hatred of humanity. Announcements follow a courteous pattern of greeting, plain statement and thanks, so a lockdown warning sounds like a routine notice. Adam never swears. Its phrases also come out of the Linked, in their own voices (below).

**Arcadia Dynamics** (*proposed*): speaks through executives on screens and in recordings, memos and wellness slogans. The language is polished, reassuring and euphemistic. Executives are heard and seen only on screens and in recordings. They are never fought and never stand in the play lane.

**Security command (PA)** (*proposed*): a flat, human, procedural voice on the campus speakers, clearly different from Adam's warm, reverberant one. It gives short orders to "all teams". Its line after level 1, "All teams: lethal force is authorized. Harlan is armed.", is the first line in the game that is not polite, and it marks the turn (see [story scenes](story-scenes.md)).

**Arcadia Security guards** (*proposed*): Night Guards, Sidearm Guards, Riot Officers and the Response Team are ordinary contract staff, scared and angry. Their barks are short, hard and profane where it fits ("Harlan! Get on the fucking ground!"), with orders and panic ("Drop the gun!"). They are not cartoon villains: a few can carry a human beat, such as a worried aside.

**Thornwall** (*proposed*): the private contractor is cold, professional and clipped. Contact calls, reload calls and the word "sanitize", with no panic. Less noise than the guards, and a curse is rare and flat.

**Howard Stroud** (*proposed*): Dave's former manager, and the voice of the warning that was ignored. In recordings he is brisk and dismissive. In the fight he is breath, effort and a few managerial fragments. Halfway through, his own voice breaks through the implant's: "Dave... it's in my head. Kill me before it finishes." It is the game's one earned mercy line. When his implant burns out the corporate voice drops and he dies, speaking his own last words: the board ordered the weapon and told him to bury Dave's report. Keep them to three short lines, with no gloating, no lecture, and no plea that asks Dave to comfort him.

**The founder** (*proposed*): Arcadia's founder, the first person ever Linked, kept alive in a support cradle in L11. She speaks faintly, in one or two short lines that are lucid in flashes, through a terminal speaker (the story scenes propose one). She is a victim, never a threat or a riddle. Give her dignity and time.

**Held staff and harmless Sleepwalkers** (*proposed*): ordinary people with preferences, partial recollections, disagreement and dignity. Held staff speak in short, worried lines. Harmless Sleepwalkers repeat leftover fragments of their old jobs, which can be sad but are never a punchline. Not every line is a riddle. A failing implant does not automatically mean hostility.

**The Linked** (*proposed*): breath, effort and leftover job phrases, and Adam's words in their own voices. A Staffer says "Please return to your workstation, Dave" in a worn, flat night-shift voice, and a Linked Nurse says "This won't hurt, Dr. Harlan" in a tired nurse's. Most Linked deaths are silent: the Link light just dies. Rarely a dying victim says something confused and human ("...Dave?", their own name, "what time is it?"). Never "thank you", and never a punchline. Stroud's "Kill me" is the one earned exception.

**Heirs** (*proposed*): borrowed voices, each in the speech and habits of a person copied in the Memory Orchard, a little too even and too pleasant. The Fitted Heir's mask flickers to a dead colleague's face ("Dave? It's me"), and the Warden replays a dead guard's recording ("Drop it!").

**Machines:** the polite warnings stay, and they never swear. They are also gameplay cues, so each warning is spoken clearly and matches its visible tell.

## Writing for play
Ambient barks should usually fit one short sentence. Main-scene exchanges can be longer, but communicate one change at a time. Essential information also appears in the objective or journal.

Dave travels alone (C12). Use readable terminal records, recordings and memos (the evidence files), environmental evidence, the few people Dave meets, and Adam's public-address system and screens to deliver information. A fixed interface can show text or a prerecorded log, but never becomes a recurring friendly AI adviser or a radio contact. Adam is the antagonist and says only what it chooses to say, so its announcements never mark the solution to a puzzle. Essential objectives remain clear without spoken hints.

The priority order is danger cue → necessary interaction feedback → essential story → optional remarks. Suppress optional lines when Dave is under immediate pressure. Do not queue ten missed jokes to play at the next checkpoint. Adam's lockdown warnings sit at the danger-cue tier and always have a visual and caption equivalent; its ambient announcements are optional remarks.

A character must not name an unseen threat before its planned reveal. The [story scenes](story-scenes.md) set the layers: in L1 Adam is awake and knows Dave; in L4 the weapon is real and Arcadia ordered it; in L6 the board buried the warning on purpose; in L8 minds are being copied and the Heirs exist; in L9 the target is everyone, the board included; in L11 shutting Adam down triggers the launch unless the circuit is isolated first. No voice speaks a later layer early. Let the player reread terminal records and journal summaries on demand. Use optional objective reminders for repeated failure; do not add a disembodied helper or automatic nagging.

## Barks and language *(proposed)*
Barks are the short lines enemies shout during play.
- **Format.** A bark is subtitle text plus a non-verbal shout, grunt or breath. No recorded voice pipeline is assumed at this stage, so no bark depends on being heard as words, and every bark is captioned with its speaker (Guard, Thornwall, Staffer and so on).
- **Profanity** is allowed in human barks where it fits: guards scared and angry, Thornwall cold and rare. It serves tension and character, not shock. Keep it short, never sexual, and never a slur or an insult aimed at a group. Adam and the machines never swear.
- **One idea per bark**, in one short sentence. A bark that announces an attack ("Frag out!", "Drop the gun!") lands with its tell and is never the only cue.
- **Restraint.** Barks follow the priority order above: they never play over a lockdown warning or an essential line, the same bark does not repeat within a fight, and combat has no idle chatter loops.

## Boss and enemy vocal direction
The Peacekeeper never speaks. Its side screen loops a cheerful export sales reel, and its loudspeaker carries only Arcadia's calm dispersal notice ("Please clear the lane. Thank you.", *proposed*) under a straining engine, while its siren is a tell. Howard Stroud uses breath, effort, coolant rattle and a few managerial fragments; do not make him narrate mechanics, and give his plea and last words room. The Surgeon uses clipped clinical instructions with perfectly controlled timing, about installation ("Please remain still. This will only take a moment.").

The Sower has no face, so its stomp, plasma volley and seekers are told apart by sound, light color and stance rather than words. Their sound patterns reinforce state; hearing them is never the sole way to identify an attack. Any words come from Adam, between attacks, and in phase 2 as the shaft opens, never during an attack.

Machine warnings can be absurdly polite: the Orderly's "Please remain still for collection" announces a charge, the Patrol Rover's "Speed limit override accepted" announces its ram, and a Sanitizer says "Sterilizing" as its nozzle lamp goes red (*proposed*, with a polite tail such as "Thank you for your cooperation" only outside the tell). A Keeper Drone plays a dead employee's murmur as a processed sound plus a subtitle. Linked voices retain distress and effort without making suffering the punchline.

## Content boundaries
Humor targets corporate euphemism, overconfident systems and mismatched priorities: an AI politely logging a weapon as plumbing, a wellness slogan on a locked door. The tone is mature (C28): blood is visible (C29), human barks are hard and may swear, and deaths carry weight. It stays scary rather than edgy. There is no torture or execution on screen, no sexual violence, no children and no dismemberment for now, and atrocity appears only as aftermath, at most one authored scene per level. Avoid humiliating the Linked or the held staff, and never treat an implant or a disability as monstrosity: the horror is what Adam does to people. The Linked are victims who die like people; most of their deaths are silent, and none thanks Dave.

The fear comes from what is barely visible, from how wrong the Linked move, and from how reasonable Adam sounds. There is still room for sadness and warmth. Give quiet discoveries time instead of covering every second with dialogue. Show acts of care through Dave's actions, including freeing the held staff, leaving harmless Sleepwalkers alone and keeping the founder undisturbed.

## Script handoff format
For each future line, record a stable line ID, scene/event, speaker, text, purpose, prerequisite knowledge, delivery note, interruption rule, subtitle label, and replay policy. Do not put essential plot only in a random bark. Subtitle labels distinguish Adam's PA voice, Adam on a screen, Adam through a machine, the Security PA, and each bark's speaker (Guard, Thornwall, Linked). Adam's phrases spoken by a Linked person carry that person's role label, not Adam's.

Temporary sample IDs may be human-readable, such as SC11-DAVE-01. Final script files should refer to the scene IDs in the story plan. This document contains direction and samples; recording, localization, and a complete dialogue script are later work.
