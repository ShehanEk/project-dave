## What the game should feel like

The player is a resourceful scavenger making progress through an unexpectedly beautiful place that keeps trying to contain them. Movement should feel responsive, weapons should have distinct physical character, and curiosity should frequently reveal something useful or human.

The confirmed loop is **explore → fight → collect treasure → overcome an obstacle → reach a checkpoint → upgrade**. Repeat it with different emphasis rather than putting a mandatory battle and purchase in every room.

## Design pillars

| Pillar | Player experience | Design consequence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Readable adventure | I can understand a threat, make a plan, and react. | Give attacks, moving platforms, and weapon swaps clear previews. |
| A meaningful weapon choice | The weapon in my hands changes my approach. | One carried weapon; all mandatory encounters support the legitimate choice. |
| Treasure with purpose | Looking around pays off. | Gems reward routes and fund proposed upgrades; artifacts provide distinctive discoveries. |
| Dangerous care | The world is dangerous for a believable reason. | Enemies and hazards reflect EDEN's original maintenance or medical functions. |
| People inside the machinery | There is more here than targets and loot. | Preserve quiet companion scenes, peaceful Rememberers, and the First Patient. |

## A typical section

A short safe view introduces the next landmark. A visible gem trail suggests a side route. One encounter tests a familiar behavior, followed by a movement obstacle with a recovery ledge. The route opens into a checkpoint, where the player can pause, assess the held weapon, and optionally upgrade it.

**Proposed pacing target:** an ordinary section lasts roughly two to four minutes before a clear change of activity. This is a writing target, not a measured requirement. Long boss fights, quiet reveals, and early tutorials can differ.

Checkpoints divide sustained pressure, not every small jump. A gem trail can cross a combat space, but it must not lure the player toward an unreadable hazard. An upgrade purchase never unlocks the next door by itself.

## Player decisions worth preserving

- Keep a familiar weapon or swap to a newly discovered one.
- Fight from a safe position or approach for stronger short-range damage.
- Take a clearly signaled optional route for gems or an artifact.
- Spend gems now on the carried type or save for another type encountered later.
- Use the tether's environmental options if it is the carried weapon, while accepting its lack of direct gunfire.

Do not simulate choice by giving one weapon a mandatory immunity counter. No early discarded gun may become a required key later.

## Scope for the first complete design

Single-player, authored twelve-level campaign, four scheduled unique mini-bosses, five weapon types with three upgrades each. No crafting tree, multiplayer, procedural campaign, random equipment rarity, companion loadout, stealth skill tree, stamina meter, or second weapon slot is assumed.

These exclusions are proposed scope boundaries, not permanent bans on future ideas. Proposing one requires documenting what existing design it changes.

## Completion criteria

A level is complete when its authored exit or story objective is reached. Fighting is necessary in selected encounters, not a universal requirement to kill every creature. Gems and artifacts do not gate the ending. A checkpoint is a safe pause, not a mandatory shop interaction.

## Review questions

Can the player name their immediate goal? Is the next danger readable? Does treasure reward deliberate attention? Does this section support every weapon that can enter it? Does the world still feel like EDEN rather than a generic combat corridor?
