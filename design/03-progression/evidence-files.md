# DEAD EDEN — Evidence files

**Direction (C14–C35, 2026-09-29):** mature dark sci-fi — evil corporation Arcadia Dynamics, sentient AI Adam, rogue AI researcher Dave Harlan; human, cyborg and machine enemies with guns; visible blood; no zombies, no stealth. ## Collection rules
Proposed roster: **one optional evidence file per level, twelve total**. Each is a memo, recording, log or photo that fits its level's story beat and proves something Arcadia or Adam did. They are additional to microchips and to mandatory story evidence, such as the partial copy of Adam's logs that Dave takes in level 1. A player may complete the campaign without collecting any of them.

Evidence files are journal-only. They add a small journal entry and a distinctive object illustration; they cannot be sold, have no stat effect, and never gate the ending. They do not occupy the weapon slot, confer combat powers, unlock mandatory doors (they are never keycards), or act as currency. Dave copies each file onto one of the evidence drives in the belt pouch. The journal is the record, and the pouch is abstracted and never shown holding full-size weapons.

Show each find in a lit terminal, recorder dock, file drawer or tray ([W03](../04-world/objects-and-hazards.md), evidence file point) with a steady pale rim light, a small square copy-port lamp, and a short unique discovery sound. Avoid gold (microchips) and amber or red (warnings and tells). Collection requires Interact and records a stable evidence-file ID. In combat, record it quietly and defer the description until the player is safe. Do not freeze the hero in a damaging animation.

The locations below are placement proposals within the existing level themes; the detailed route briefs still need exact alcove allocation. Each can be reached with baseline movement and ordinary switches. A tether shortcut may exist, but tether ownership is not required to complete the collection. None holds the level's keycard, which stays on the main route.

Two of them carry the arms-sale thread that Arcadia's defense division sells guns and trucks to the world: the Peacekeeper's export contract (EF03) and Thornwall's invoice (EF05). No file shows a body or an injury.

## Image and model handoff
For each evidence file, create one neutral isolated object, a strict side silhouette, and a readable 2D pickup pose. Start each copy-ready prompt with the standard opening in the [style guide](../../art-design/style-guide.md). Keep lettering out of the generated drawing; small inscriptions can be represented by abstract lines until readable text is designed separately.

Use Arcadia's world materials: matte polymer, brushed steel, coated card stock and photographic paper, with restrained wear and confident dark outlines. Keep most items around palm to forearm size. Distinguish silhouette first; avoid twelve identical glowing chips with different colors. Pickup glows are effects, not permanent geometry.

## Completion and saving
A journal shows discovered entries and undiscovered level slots without revealing solutions. Collecting all twelve gives a completion acknowledgement and optional gallery, not a stronger weapon, alternate mandatory ending, or thirteenth level. The final news broadcast may mention the files Dave found; that is cosmetic only and changes nothing about the ending.

Evidence files follow checkpoint rollback exactly. A unique record and its world pickup commit together. Keep optional flavor text short: roughly 30–60 words per item in a future writing pass (a recording is a short transcript excerpt). This catalog establishes objects and meaning; it is not yet the final written journal.
