## Established foundation

The hero finds the desired power core inside a talkative maintenance robot. The robot needs to reach the facility's depths. It knows EDEN's procedures and initially defends them. Its core ultimately carries original shutdown authority, revealed in level 11.

## Proposed identity

**Working name:** PIP, short for Portable Infrastructure Partner. A small maintenance guide whose optimism is partly personality and partly a habit of reporting every malfunction as manageable.

PIP's initial objective is to reach central diagnostics and correct a growing list of service faults. It regards the trip as a maintenance visit until evidence forces it to question the care program itself.

PIP is a person in the story, not a bag of spare weapons. Rook's attempted core extraction is interrupted before lasting injury.

## Proposed visual design

A 0.65 m pear-shaped cream shell hovers slightly above the ground on a visible compact lift grille. A large oval dark face panel contains one expressive cyan eye and a small speaker slit. Two short folding utility arms sit on the sides, each with three padded digits.

A protected amber core window occupies the lower chest. It suggests value without resembling a collectible gem floating loose inside the body. One mint service stripe wraps the shell. A short rear handle and two small folded landing feet support workshop poses.

Keep PIP's single eye, rounded body, and hovering posture distinct from the three-wheeled, toolbox-shaped enemy Patchbot. PIP's arms are not weapon barrels.

## Gameplay role and limits

- Gives concise context and occasional optional hints.
- Operates designated story consoles with the hero's interaction.
- Fits purchased upgrade modules at authored maintenance checkpoints and reapplies already-earned fittings when a matching physical weapon is picked up.
- Remembers purchased upgrade designs as information, not a stored arsenal.
- Comments on artifacts after discovery, without revealing every secret automatically.
- Identifies the life-support dependency and authority interface when the level-11 terminal exposes them.

PIP does not shoot, heal during combat, collect distant gems, revive the hero, carry a second gun, shield attacks, solve platforming automatically, or open every door. No companion skill tree is proposed.

## Following and accessibility

The proposed baseline treats PIP as nonblocking and not independently attackable. It stays out of firing lanes and important platform edges. It may use background service ducts to catch up when the authored route makes literal following awkward.

This catch-up behavior cannot move the hero, retrieve a dropped gun, trigger inaccessible controls, or become a free traversal shortcut. In quiet scenes PIP moves closer; in combat it remains visually secondary.

A companion-location marker appears only when its position matters for a story interaction. The player never loses progress because a follower gets stuck.

## Authority reveal

The authority credentials are old, sealed, and only recognized by the original care terminal. PIP does not knowingly conceal a universal solution for ten levels. Recognition explains the core's value and the companion's unusual access, but shutdown remains dangerous until support is separated.

PIP's core stays installed during the ending. It connects through a service interface; the game does not require killing the companion to save everyone else.

## Sprite and reference requirements

Separate shell, face panel, one eye display, core window, two arms, lift grille, handle, and landing feet. Reference states: neutral hover, cautious look, proud explanation, nervous correction, console connection, upgrade fitting, landing, and final relieved silence.
