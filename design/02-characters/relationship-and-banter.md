## Proposed relationship arc

| Campaign section | Rook's view of PIP | PIP's view of EDEN | Relationship change |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Levels 1–3 | Valuable machinery with inconvenient opinions | Procedures are probably correct | Mutual usefulness becomes reluctant trust. |
| Levels 4–6 | A capable partner with selective optimism | Local systems have serious faults | PIP starts admitting uncertainty. |
| Levels 7–9 | Someone whose loyalty deserves honesty | Care can violate the people it serves | Both begin acting for survivors rather than an easy exit. |
| Levels 10–12 | A person, not a saleable core | Authority needs limits and evidence | They choose to preserve life and agency together. |

Names in this table are working proposals. Preserve the relationship shape if either name changes.

## A bond shown through actions

Early Rook reaches for the core before asking the robot's name. Later they protect PIP's access to a console because they trust its judgment. In the final scene the core is still valuable, but Rook no longer treats that as the important fact.

PIP initially corrects terminology: escape becomes discharge, zombies become patients, and attacks become interventions. Over time it uses simpler and more honest descriptions. Its growth is visible in word choice, not a new combat power.

Do not make every encounter trigger an emotional confession. Use small acts, brief corrections, and quiet reactions between major scenes.

## Banter categories

- **Orientation:** identify a visible destination without solving every obstacle.
- **World reaction:** respond to a strange medical or maintenance behavior.
- **Character friction:** Rook questions a procedure; PIP attempts to defend it.
- **Discovery:** one short exchange about an artifact or memory.
- **Trust:** a rare acknowledgement after a difficult shared event.

Attack warnings take priority over banter. Pause or suppress casual speech during a new boss tell, dense platforming, or a player interaction with a menu.

## Proposed trigger rules

An authored line plays once per campaign checkpoint state, with a short replay option in the dialogue log. A failed boss attempt does not repeat its whole introduction. Optional idle lines have long spacing and a limited pool; silence is an intended outcome.

Hints should follow repeated difficulty or an explicit request, not fire the moment the hero enters a room. Offer an observation first, then a clearer hint if requested. Never reveal a hidden artifact simply because the companion can theoretically scan.

## Sample voice progression

Early:
- Rook: “That flower has a barrel.”
- PIP: “An unusually assertive pollen outlet.”

Middle:
- Rook: “You said this corridor was safe.”
- PIP: “The report is old. I should have said that.”

Late:
- PIP: “The system says they responded.”
- Rook: “Did anybody ask what they wanted?”

These lines are draft examples, not final scripts or repeated combat barks.

## Guardrails

Humor should target malfunctioning procedure, bureaucracy, and the pair's assumptions. Avoid jokes about patients' suffering or making PIP deliberately useless. Rook need not become perfectly selfless, and PIP need not reject all technology.

The relationship has no approval meter, romance system, dialogue skill check, or branching loyalty ending in the current proposal. Its progression follows the twelve-level story.
