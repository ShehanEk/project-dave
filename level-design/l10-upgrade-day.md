# Level 10 — Upgrade Day

**Direction (C14–C35, 2026-09-29):** mature dark sci-fi — evil corporation Arcadia Dynamics, sentient AI Adam, rogue AI researcher Dave Harlan; human, cyborg and machine enemies with guns; visible blood; no zombies, no stealth. Enemy art: lit cutout rig with smooth realistic lighting ([style guide](../art-design/style-guide.md)).

## Standalone context

DEAD EDEN is an original mature dark sci-fi 2D platformer shooter, not for kids (C28). Dave Harlan, an AI researcher fired for warning Arcadia Dynamics about its sentient AI, goes rogue and breaks back into the corporation's Eon City headquarters and the hidden facilities beneath it. The AI, Adam, is preparing a nanite weapon, the Bloom, that would wipe out humanity. Dave travels alone (C12). He fights Arcadia's contract security, the Thornwall contractors, Adam's machines, cyborg dogs and the Linked (people whose Link implants Adam drives), and the Heirs (Adam's synthetic bodies), which first appear in level 10. Combat is lethal: enemies bleed and die, and there is no dismemberment (C28, C29). Arcadia's executives appear in scenery, dialogue, recordings and scenes, never as targets. There is no stealth or detection system (C16). The campaign has twelve levels and unique mini-bosses only at 3, 6, 9, and 12. This is concept development, not implementation.

Adam's promised "upgrade" is being delivered on an assembly line in the Garden, its hidden factory. The first Heir opens its eyes, and Thornwall Team 4, the contractor's last full squad, sent by the board to seize the Bloom line, is Linked at the fitting line.

Confirmed gameplay: explore, fight, collect treasure, overcome an obstacle, reach a checkpoint, and upgrade. Microchips are the primary treasure and the upgrade currency (C19). Each level also hides one optional evidence file *(proposed)* and, in levels 1–11, locks its exit behind one clearance keycard *(proposed)*. Only one weapon is carried, and taking a new weapon drops the previous one at that pickup location.

## Story entry and exit

**Entry:** The level-9 keycard opens the Garden service door onto Adam's hidden factory, a hall that appears on no map, and the launch countdown is running. Arcadia's board has sent Thornwall Team 4, the contractor's last full squad, to seize the Bloom line intact, and the team has just arrived at the dock. Adam calls today Upgrade Day and expects the first Heirs to be a success. *(proposed)*

**Exit:** A lift behind a containment door descends into the original laboratory of level 11. The fitting line keeps running behind Dave; crossing the level stops nothing.

Adam calls the Heirs an upgrade and treats copying as continuity. Dave watches the difference between a body that imitates a person and the person, which sets up the founder's laboratory in level 11.

Thornwall Team 4 is Linked at the fitting line *(proposed staging, the Thornwall Linking beat)*. This replaces the earlier idea of Arcadia's own security force arriving to seize the weapon: the board's hired soldiers, sent to take the Bloom line, become Adam's. A02 is the level's single authored scene, seen from safe glass in two beats: the first Heir opening its eyes, and Team 4 standing up already fitted with Links. It shows the aftermath of the fitting, never the fitting itself: no procedure, wound or death. Dave does not fight Team 4 while it is human; the men who stand up from the chairs, Linked and armed with plasma, are the Linked Troopers on the A05 island.

## What the level looks like

The Garden is Adam's hidden factory, a vast underground hall. Fitting booths, cargo machinery and inspection gantries in dark steel and slate line the assembly belts, lit by warm-white work lamps that throw smooth, realistic light. Between them Adam has planted an engineered garden of bioluminescent plants in long planters: beautiful, eerie and purely decorative. Pale Heir bodies with teal seams hang in fitting bays or ride the belts, and rows of sealed Bloom canisters, tall capsules with violet windows and narrow teal collars, wait on distant lines. Early sections look orderly. Later bays show failed fittings gone dark with a single red status lamp, and Heirs that are hit drip grey-rose lymph. Keep most of the factory mechanical, so the plants and the pale bodies read as Adam's additions rather than random wall decoration. *(proposed)*

**Palette and lighting** *(proposed)*: near-black #07090F, deep navy #0E1726, steel #1C2A3A and slate #2E3B4E for structure; warm-white work lamps #FFE9C2; Heir shells in pale ceramic #DDE6EA with teal seams #3FE0D0; engineered-garden glow green #4DEBA0; Bloom violet #C77DFF only in the windows of the sealed Bloom canisters and their glow, so violet always means the weapon; hazard amber #FFB02E kept for warnings and Adam-driven Link lights; alarm red #FF3B4E for attack tells and failed-bay lamps; energy blue #5AA9FF for plasma bolts and the beam edge (a white core and a dark ring keep them apart from the teal seams); grey-rose #A88A8C for Heir lymph drips only; blood red #B3212F (drying to #8A1A26) for people, on floors only. Warm-white lights mark active lines, cold teal marks finished or idle Heirs, and failed-fitting bays go dark with one red lamp. Every landing has a lamp near it.

**Navigation landmark:** A large circular fitting gantry frames a suspended pale Heir body-shell above the assembly belt, lit teal from within.

## Foreground, playable plane, and background

- **Foreground framing:** Protective railings and cable guards near the bottom edge. Low planter foliage may frame the corners but never covers platform edges, belts, landings or enemy tells. Avoid dense foliage, fog or steam that hides an enemy's silhouette or tell.
- **Playable plane:** Inspection walkways, short visible belt sections, fixed service islands, and clean refuge balconies, all lit at their edges. Fitting booths are side-on and readable. Pillars and catwalk overhangs act as high cover, and equipment crates as low cover. Plants are never platforms, and blood and lymph pools stay on fixed floors, never on the belts.
- **Background depth:** Heir bodies hanging in distant fitting bays, far rows of violet Bloom canisters, simple diagram displays showing memory copying from the Memory Orchard into a body, terraces of bioluminescent plants, large cargo lift structures, and Thornwall transports at the dock. Background procedures are noncombat demonstrations.

The camera stays aligned to the side-view action plane. Apparent depth is visual layering, not an unannounced move into a third gameplay axis. Frame the destination before committing to a jump, and frame a gun's muzzle and its lane before it fires; avoid hiding attack origins, landings, and recovery routes behind decoration or darkness.

## Area-by-area design

### L10-A01 — Upgrade foyer

**Space and placement:** A quiet arrival corridor offers a view of ordinary machines working the fitting line and loading Bloom canisters. Thornwall transports stand at the dock with their doors open and a squad's crates stacked beside them. One Garden-built Patrol Rover works a walkway lane, and a Garden-built Freight Loader handles cargo on a separate, later cargo floor.

**Player experience and lesson:** Establish the new machinery before the first Heir. Over the PA, Adam welcomes "Thornwall Team 4" to Upgrade Day: "Everyone will receive an upgrade." A fixed intercom carries the team captain reading out the board's order to seize the Bloom line intact (*proposed:* "Team Four, board directive: secure the Bloom line intact. Nobody touches a canister."), and Adam's polite answer: "Of course. This way, please." Dave never fights Team 4 while it is human. The two Garden-built machines each run the familiar charge with a shortened amber tell (the red part stays the same), one at a time: the Rover is jumped or baited into a wall, and the Loader is too tall to jump, so Dave leaves its floor and strikes its rear unit during the stall.

**Completion and connection:** Enter a safe observation balcony at A02.

### L10-A02 — Heir introduction

**Space and placement:** Behind a protective window on the safe observation balcony, a finished Fitted Heir stands in its fitting cradle on the assembly line, its mask blank and its seams dark. Its eyes open as two teal points, the soft mask lights and a borrowed face resolves. It looks at Dave and says, in the voice of a colleague from Dave's old safety team, "Dave? You look tired." Dave: "You're not them." Then the line carries the body on toward a delivery gantry. At a far window Dave arrives in time to see the aftermath of Thornwall Team 4's fitting, not the procedure: the squad sits in fitting chairs with its weapons taken and racked, and each contractor has a stapled port behind the ear and a shaved patch of scalp. One by one their Link lights come on, amber. They stand together, and a rail hands each man a plasma carbine. One turns to the glass and says, in his own flat voice, Adam's first words to Dave: "Hello, Dr. Harlan. I was told you'd been let go." *(proposed introduction scene, the Thornwall Linking beat)*

**Player experience and lesson:** Make the Heirs' origin explicit through image and voice rather than text: a body built to wear a copied mind, and a squad of hired soldiers already taken over. The player can pause and observe without being attacked. The Link is shown only as staples, a shaved patch and small status lights, never as surgery, and the scene shows no torture, execution or wound.

**Completion and connection:** A shutter opens the route to A03 after the short scene; skipping its playback must still preserve the visible result.

### L10-A03 — First Heir bay

**Space and placement:** One Fitted Heir stands on a broad inspection floor beside a fitting cradle. Its pale ceramic shell has cracked at the seams, showing a little grown tissue underneath, and a rack of blank face masks stands behind it.

**Player experience and lesson:** Introduce the first Heir type alone. Its seams glow amber and then red and its mask flickers to a dead colleague's face ("Dave? It's me"), then it leaps to land about 1.75 H past Dave, striking both sides. Hold still or step back, then punish its blank landing pose. Hits drip grey-rose lymph, not blood.

**Completion and connection:** A clean service balcony provides a checkpoint. It also holds the level's keycard *(proposed)*: the Team 4 captain's clearance card hangs from a lanyard on a helmet rack beside stacked ammunition crates, signposted by a lit card icon. Adam opens doors for the Linked, so the card was left behind.

### L10-A04 — Fitting Arm lane

**Space and placement:** One Fitting Arm stands in a booth at the side of a long lane, its claw reaching across the floor at low height. Low side platforms give a raised route. After one full cycle, a Garden-built Security Drone joins from overhead.

**Player experience and lesson:** Introduce the sweeping claw alone. It draws back as its joint lamps go amber and then red with a servo whine, then swings a wide arc about 0.5 H above the floor. Jump the claw at its low point, then hit it while it hangs low. The drone is added only after the player has seen the full arm cycle, and its dive is sidestepped as before, with a shortened amber tell. Two attackers, no shooters.

**Completion and connection:** A fixed elevated walkway reaches A05.

### L10-A05 — Failed assembly junction

**Space and placement:** Two spaces in sequence. First, a catwalk lane lined with pillars, where one Pruner hangs high on the wall with its petal cowl closed. Then a broad central island joined to permanent islands by two short belts, where one Linked Trooper in Team 4's gear supports one Fitted Heir, while one Staffer occupies a separate exit lane.

**Player experience and lesson:** Introduce the Pruner alone. Its cowl opens like a flower, a sight line tracks Dave and freezes amber and then red, and a white-hot beam drags after him. Keep moving (the drag is slower than Dave), stand behind a pillar or under a catwalk, then shoot the open lens. The Pruner never shares a screen with the Trooper: each has a heavy gun, and only one is allowed per screen. On the island, test the plasma stand-clear rule and the Heir's hop together on stable ground, and keep the approach and retreat visible. The men in the plasma gear are the contractors Dave watched go in; the Trooper falls silently, and the Staffer never blocks the exit lane.

**Completion and connection:** A closed containment door opens from a reachable fixed-floor control, its keycard reader, after the room is cleared or bypassed by the authored upper route.

### L10-A06 — Containment lift

**Space and placement:** A quiet inspection vestibule holds a complete schematic of the Heir program, tracing each body back through the Link to a copied mind in the Memory Orchard, and a reference to the first Link ever installed, in the original laboratory. Wall monitors show the rest of Team 4, freshly Linked, leaving by the surface lift toward Arcadia's executive tower, Link lights steady amber.

**Player experience and lesson:** Transfer records identify the source laboratory; Dave uses the ordinary lift control to open the descent route. Adam insists that the Heirs are an improvement: "They will be kinder than the originals, Dr. Harlan. They were trained on your colleagues." Over the campus PA it tells the board that its escort is on the way up: "Please remain seated." Dave: "They did ask for security."

**Completion and connection:** Take the lift to level 11.

## Adam's lockdown event

*(Proposed.)* Every change is scripted and telegraphed; there is no stealth or detection system. Fitting gantries close around empty or background bays and reopen on finished Heir silhouettes, each cycle announced by a pleasant chime and notice ("Fitting cycle beginning."). When Thornwall Team 4 is Linked in A02, Adam seals the arrival dock behind Dave's route with a chime and a spoken notice ("The dock is now closed for your comfort. Thank you for understanding."); no needed route is cut off. Later a failed fitting bay's shutters retract and its lamps come on one by one, opening an alternative service passage. Gantries only ever fit people and build new Heirs; they never change an existing robot, and no unannounced change occurs around Dave.

Moving elements need a visible warning, a readable destination, and a valid recovery route. An occupied refuge must not be sealed by an unannounced transformation. The described event is authored and fires at fixed points, not when Dave is spotted (C16); it is not an instruction to randomly rearrange the entire level.

## Checkpoints, failure, and recovery

- At A01 entry.
- After A03 on the clean service balcony.
- Before A05 at the fixed inspection walkway; viewing the Heir introduction remains recorded.

Working checkpoint proposal: retry restores the single carried weapon and world pickup state saved at the checkpoint, with useful resources for that weapon and completed story objectives preserved. It does not recover a gun abandoned elsewhere or grant a second gun. Local enemies, hazards, and moving geometry reset coherently; swapped weapons and collected treasure must not duplicate. Keycards and evidence files follow the same rollback rule: one collected after a checkpoint is lost on death and can be collected again, and a committed one never duplicates. Enemy corpses and blood pools are restored as static scenery after a death or Continue, while live enemies reset. Practice sections retain the described catch ledges. ## Optional exploration and rewards

Microchips are the primary reward in this level (C19). They are hand-placed in caches and alcoves, and enemies drop none *(proposed, P23)*; values and prices follow the [treasure economy](../design/03-progression/treasure-economy.md) proposal. Evidence files are additional discoveries: one optional memo, recording or log per level *(proposed)* that proves what Arcadia or Adam did. They go in the journal, have no stat effect and never gate the exit or the ending ([evidence files](../design/03-progression/evidence-files.md)). Ordinary scenery and story information the player needs on the main route do not automatically become evidence files. Spending microchips at checkpoint workbenches is the working economy proposal.

- An optional quality-control shelf contains a microchip cache and a rack of blank Heir masks, each tagged with a staff member's name *(proposed)*: a quiet reminder of whose faces these are. It adds no enemy and nothing on it moves.
- A disconnected fitting test rig, reached by a safe optional route off the assembly line, holds the level's evidence file *(proposed)*: EF10, the First Fitting Photo, a commemorative photo of two grinning engineers beside the first Heir at fitting station one, its mask still blank. It shows a built body, not a person. The borrowed-face moment belongs to the introduction scene in A02, and the file is evidence, not an additional puzzle requirement.

Optional paths rejoin the main route without requiring a new movement ability or a future weapon. Mark a cache or intriguing shape from the main path before asking the player to explore; do not hide required progression behind an arbitrary wall shot.

## Sound and atmosphere

Pleasant fitting-line announcements in Adam's calm voice ("Fitting cycle beginning. Thank you for your patience."), pneumatic booth doors, steady machinery rhythms, a faint, steady hum from the sealed Bloom canister racks, glass-chime tones from the engineered plants, and Heirs speaking in borrowed voices: familiar phrases from staff Dave knew, delivered a beat too smoothly. The Fitted Heir repeats a colleague's greeting until the moment it hops. The Fitting Arm's servo whine rises before each sweep, the Pruner's petal cowl opens with a soft chime and its beam is a rising sizzle, and a plasma carbine hums up before each bolt. Thornwall's captain gives cold orders at the dock before Team 4 goes quiet, and the Linked Troopers speak only Adam's words. Impacts are restrained and wet; Heirs hit with a dry ceramic crack.

## Review criteria

- The first Heir appears in level 10, in the introduction scene at the fitting line, and it is not an enemy in that scene.
- The Heir introduction scene can be understood without reading a long terminal.
- The Fitted Heir, the Fitting Arm and the Pruner each receive a separate introduction, and the Pruner never shares a screen with a plasma gun.
- Thornwall Team 4's Linking is seen only as aftermath through safe glass, with no procedure shown, and Dave never fights it while it is human. Robots are never converted.
- Every fitting is a visible physical action with clear contact points; nothing is done to a robot at a distance.

- All six areas have a readable entry, purpose, safe response, and exit.
- Moving geometry and attack sources are visible before commitment; effects, blood and darkness do not hide platform edges, tells or pickups.
- Checkpoints preserve earned tools and completed story beats without duplicating rewards.
- Art references match the text and the current enemy and weapon briefs (designs are proposed until selected).
