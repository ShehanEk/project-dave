# Choir Unit

**Asset ID:** T03\
**Category:** returned\
**First appearance:** Level 11\
**Design status:** Proposed visual direction for the established concept. Dimensions are provisional art proportions, not engine specifications.

## Identity and role

Ordinary multi-mind Returned enemy; a smaller relative in concept, not a miniature copy of the final mini-boss.

## Scale and silhouette

1.60 m body height, hovering about 0.40 m above the ground.

A floating bell-shaped chassis with two short articulated arms and three narrow face-like speaker slots arranged vertically on the front. A compact dome encloses the neural reservoir.

Use a neutral 1.70 m human silhouette as a temporary scale reference on a separate comparison sheet. The hero's final design is not fixed by this measurement.

## Appearance and construction

The dome has three separate translucent chambers arranged front-to-back inside one housing, connected to three corresponding front slots. Each slot has a single simple aperture shape: horizontal bar, circle, or vertical slit. The right arm carries a short pulse emitter; the left is a stabilizing claw. Coral neural cords are contained inside purposeful openings rather than draped everywhere.

## Color and materials

Pale ceramic #E0DBD0; desaturated blue #6E899A; soft coral tissue #CA8792; dim violet #A18EBE; dark openings #414450.

Color values are palette targets for later material work; image generators may approximate them. Pair important cues with geometry, posture, and contrast so they do not depend on color alone.

## Abilities and movement

Several recovered minds compete for control of its weapons. Proposed regular-enemy behavior: a short directed pulse volley followed by an interruption pause; no full three-phase boss routine.

Hovers with small disagreements in tilt. One voice slot brightens and opens before the emitter fires; another interrupts, causing a visible hesitation. The arms briefly point in competing directions.

## Openings and limitations

The hesitation exposes an opening to attack the lightly armored lower chassis. The dome is readable anatomy, not an automatic one-shot switch.

## Parts to keep separate for modeling

Bell chassis; dome; three contained neural chambers; three face slots; two arms; one pulse emitter; one stabilizing claw; lower lift assembly.

Preserve articulation clearance in the neutral pose. Keep projectiles, attack trails, glow cards, impact effects, and environmental props separate from the core model. A part list describes visual assembly, not manufacturing internals.

## Required pose and state references

Neutral hover; one voice taking control; aiming volley; competing voices; interrupted pause; hit reaction; disabled.

Make these as separate studies after the neutral design is approved. Use the approved neutral image as a reference so poses do not silently change anatomy or equipment.

## Consistency rules

Exactly three vertical voice slots, two arms, no legs, no full human faces growing outside the body. Do not use the arch frame or six arms of the Unfinished Choir.

Anatomical left and right refer to the subject's own sides, not the viewer's. Do not automatically mirror an asymmetrical design when generating the opposite view.
