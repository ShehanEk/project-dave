# Mr. Mulch — Grand Marshal of Gardening

**Asset ID:** B01\
**Category:** mini-bosses\
**First appearance:** Level 3\
**Design status:** Proposed visual direction for the established concept. Dimensions are provisional art proportions, not engine specifications.

## Identity and role

First mini-boss; a purely mechanical parade-float mower that teaches dodge-and-punish combat.

## Scale and silhouette

2.50 m tall and 3.80 m long; approximately one and a half hero heights.

A long low mower chassis on four chunky wheels supporting a bulbous smiling mascot head and a rigid festival canopy. The front cutting deck is a broad rounded wedge; a ribbed motor protrudes at the back.

Use a neutral 1.70 m human silhouette as a temporary scale reference on a separate comparison sheet. The hero's final design is not fixed by this measurement.

## Appearance and construction

Make the mascot face a molded panel with two crescent eyes and a fixed wide smile. Two rigid leaf-shaped decorations flank the canopy. The front deck has three protected spinning drums visible only from a low inset opening. A side chute throws grass clippings. Two rear cooling shutters cover part of the motor until a collision jolts them open. A striped bunting strip is attached along the canopy edge.

## Color and materials

Fresh garden green #76AD50; cream #F0E2BD; parade coral #E68C72; honey gold #E7B94E; dark tire rubber #37423A. Keep bright decoration above muddy lower mechanics.

Color values are palette targets for later material work; image generators may approximate them. Pair important cues with geometry, posture, and contrast so they do not depend on color alone.

## Abilities and movement

Charges toward the hero and separately sweeps low clippings across the arena. In phase two it chains two signaled charges. No summoned enemies.

Bounces cheerfully on suspension, dips the cutting deck with a clear mechanical clunk, announces a charge, and accelerates. On collision its upper mascot wobbles and rear shutters spring open while it reverses.

## Openings and limitations

Rear motor after a planter collision. Both side views must show how a player behind it can see and hit this component.

## Parts to keep separate for modeling

Chassis; four wheels and suspension; hinged cutting deck; three inset drums; fixed mascot head; canopy; two leaf decorations; chute; rear motor; two shutters; simple bunting.

Preserve articulation clearance in the neutral pose. Keep projectiles, attack trails, glow cards, impact effects, and environmental props separate from the core model. A part list describes visual assembly, not manufacturing internals.

## Required pose and state references

Neutral parade; deck lowered charge tell; charge; planter impact; reverse with motor exposed; clippings sweep; phase-two warning; disabled.

Make these as separate studies after the neutral design is approved. Use the approved neutral image as a reference so poses do not silently change anatomy or equipment.

## Consistency rules

No flesh, autonomous satellite units, humanoid legs, or living eyes. The smiling head is decoration on a machine, not a second character.

Anatomical left and right refer to the subject's own sides, not the viewer's. Do not automatically mirror an asymmetrical design when generating the opposite view.

## Arena relationship and phase changes

A broad garden plaza with low jump platforms and reinforced planters. Show the arena only on a separate context sheet; one planter-impact interaction is enough to explain the fight.

Phase one keeps one attack active at a time. Phase two uses a doubled charge announcement and faster wheel motion, not a new body form. Damage may bend bunting and open shutters but must preserve the same chassis.
