# Old Rootjaw — Keeper of the Pump

**Asset ID:** B02\
**Category:** mini-bosses\
**First appearance:** Level 6\
**Design status:** Proposed visual direction for the established concept. Dimensions are provisional art proportions, not engine specifications.

## Identity and role

Second mini-boss; a biological root-mutated worker controlling the arena floor.

## Scale and silhouette

3.60 m from visible root base to head; main body about 2.80 m wide. Attached roots can extend beyond the character's bounds.

A huge stooped human torso fused into a root pedestal, with two arm-like root limbs, a broad jaw, and a hunched collar of thick woody growth. There are no separate walking legs.

Use a neutral 1.70 m human silhouette as a temporary scale reference on a separate comparison sheet. The hero's final design is not fixed by this measurement.

## Appearance and construction

A stretched mustard work apron and a fragment of a green shirt identify the former worker. The face retains two tired eyes above a heavy bark-framed jaw. Three blunt seed vents grow along the upper back. A pale oval growth sits in the chest and becomes visible when the arms lift. Exactly four main anchor roots leave the base and wrap around separate pump fixtures.

## Color and materials

Weathered bark #786143; moss green #718651; ochre apron #B39449; pale chest growth #D8BB91; muted mauve living skin #A79191. Use dry wood outside and softer intact tissue at joints.

Color values are palette targets for later material work; image generators may approximate them. Pair important cues with geometry, posture, and contrast so they do not depend on color alone.

## Abilities and movement

Root slams attack warned platform positions; back vents release seed volleys. Later pulls one central platform underwater while leaving safe side routes.

Leans against the pump as if exhausted. Root pulses travel outward before a floor slam. After striking, the arms pull against the ground and lift the chest long enough to expose its vulnerable growth.

## Openings and limitations

The oval chest growth during recovery. The pump is trapped environmental machinery, not a robot body or a mechanical heart.

## Parts to keep separate for modeling

Main torso and head; two root arms; root pedestal; four anchor roots; three seed vents; chest growth; clothing remnants. Pump, pipes, platforms, and reservoir remain separate environment assets.

Preserve articulation clearance in the neutral pose. Keep projectiles, attack trails, glow cards, impact effects, and environmental props separate from the core model. A part list describes visual assembly, not manufacturing internals.

## Required pose and state references

Resting on pump; root pulse warning; arm lift; ground slam; chest-exposed recovery; seed volley; platform pull; defeated slump.

Make these as separate studies after the neutral design is approved. Use the approved neutral image as a reference so poses do not silently change anatomy or equipment.

## Consistency rules

Two main arms, four anchor roots, three seed vents. No metal skeleton, robot face, extra human heads, or exposed organs. Lower body is root mass, not hidden legs.

Anatomical left and right refer to the subject's own sides, not the viewer's. Do not automatically mirror an asymmetrical design when generating the opposite view.

## Arena relationship and phase changes

Three raised platforms over a root-filled reservoir, permanent side ledges, and tether anchors. Diagrams should show the body, root paths, and pump as distinct objects.

The second phase changes root poses and arena platform positions. It does not add an untelegraphed new limb or transform the pump into an infected robot.

## Before modeling

- Approve one neutral design as the visual master; keep all later images tied to it.
- Compare the same mechanical seams, anatomy, attachment sides, and part counts across every view.
- Check the side silhouette at small gameplay size and in grayscale; important targets and attack poses must remain readable.
- Block out the main forms and test the required poses before adding small surface details. Resolve conflicts between generated views deliberately rather than averaging them blindly.
- Treat the turnaround as concept reference. It is not a guaranteed dimensionally consistent blueprint or a ready-to-use 3D mesh.
