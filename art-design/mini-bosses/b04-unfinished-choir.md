# The Unfinished Choir — EDEN's Accepted Patient

**Asset ID:** B04\
**Category:** mini-bosses\
**First appearance:** Level 12\
**Design status:** Proposed visual direction for the established concept. Dimensions are provisional art proportions, not engine specifications.

## Identity and role

Fourth and hardest mini-boss; a unique Returned prototype with three competing identities.

## Scale and silhouette

3.50 m tall; 2.40 m wide at rest; arm spread up to 4.70 m.

A tall arch-shaped machine frame on two powerful jointed legs, with exactly six arms in three pairs and three full mask plates arranged horizontally above a central torso chamber. Its broad arch and grounded legs distinguish it from ordinary floating Choir Units.

Use a neutral 1.70 m human silhouette as a temporary scale reference on a separate comparison sheet. The hero's final design is not fixed by this measurement.

## Appearance and construction

Three mask plates represent the Warden, Hunger, and Caretaker. Anatomical right mask has a horizontal bar aperture, center mask a round mouth, and left mask a vertical slot. Upper arms bear broad guard panels, each with one inset pulse emitter along its outer edge; middle arms end in heavy gripping claws; lower arms carry repair couplers. A central three-segment shell covers intact coral neural tissue. Thick organic bands pass through visible installed interface sockets. The back has a recessed power port exposed during turns.

## Color and materials

Aged cream #DED7C4; dark desaturated teal #3F6769; coral neural tissue #C27D8B; violet shadows #8F7EA9. State accents: cyan Warden, amber Hunger, lilac Caretaker, always paired with posture and aperture shape.

Color values are palette targets for later material work; image generators may approximate them. Pair important cues with geometry, posture, and contrast so they do not depend on color alone.

## Abilities and movement

Warden fires disciplined marked attacks and exposes the rear port while turning. Hunger leaps and exposes living tissue on landing. Caretaker connects to an external repair node; interruption opens the torso. Later phases overlap a lingering hazard with the next identity and move central platforms.

The Warden straightens the body and raises the guard pair; the Hunger drops the shoulders, spreads the claw pair, and crouches before a leap; the Caretaker lowers the guard panels and extends the coupler pair toward a repair node. Inactive arms fold back in repeatable resting positions.

## Openings and limitations

State-specific rear port, landing-exposed tissue, and opened central chamber. These are distinct geometry states of the same body, not new weak points appearing without explanation.

## Parts to keep separate for modeling

Arch frame; three fixed-position masks with moving shutters; six arms in three functional pairs; two inset guard-panel emitters; two legs; three torso shell segments; neural chamber and thick bands; rear port. Repair node, hazards, and platforms remain separate.

Preserve articulation clearance in the neutral pose. Keep projectiles, attack trails, glow cards, impact effects, and environmental props separate from the core model. A part list describes visual assembly, not manufacturing internals.

## Required pose and state references

Neutral folded arms; Warden guard and turn; Hunger crouch and landing; Caretaker link; exposed torso; contested control transition; final-phase damage; disabled.

Make these as separate studies after the neutral design is approved. Use the approved neutral image as a reference so poses do not silently change anatomy or equipment.

## Consistency rules

Exactly three masks, six arms, two legs. Masks never swap anatomical position. Do not generate extra heads in the tissue, a floating bell body, or a generic giant Choir Unit.

Anatomical left and right refer to the subject's own sides, not the viewer's. Do not automatically mirror an asymmetrical design when generating the opposite view.

## Arena relationship and phase changes

Suspended platforms, fixed recovery ledges, marked tether anchors, and one visible repair node within the core chamber. Use a separate arena sheet so the character stays readable.

Phase one teaches identities separately; phase two retains one hazard into the next attack; phase three shortens transitions and shifts central platforms, with at most two simultaneous threats. Visual damage reveals the existing tissue; it never silently adds new limbs.
