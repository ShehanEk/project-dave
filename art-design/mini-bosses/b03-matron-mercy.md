# Matron Mercy — Director of Discharge

**Asset ID:** B03\
**Category:** mini-bosses\
**First appearance:** Level 9\
**Design status:** Proposed visual direction for the established concept. Dimensions are provisional art proportions, not engine specifications.

## Identity and role

Third mini-boss; an entirely mechanical surgical director with coordinated support systems.

## Scale and silhouette

3.00 m central body height; up to 4.40 m span with all arms spread.

An upright bell-shaped surgical chassis suspended from a visible overhead gimbal, with one small oval head and exactly six arms arranged in three left-right pairs. Her silhouette resembles a carefully arranged surgical workstation.

Use a neutral 1.70 m human silhouette as a temporary scale reference on a separate comparison sheet. The hero's final design is not fixed by this measurement.

## Appearance and construction

The upper arm pair carries surgical light and laser heads; the middle pair carries chunky syringe launchers; the lower pair carries padded manipulation clamps. The head has two level eyes and a thin horizontal mouth grille. A circular chest door protects the controller. The top gimbal connects to a separate ceiling rail. Three care stations are separate arena props, not attached extra limbs.

## Color and materials

Porcelain white #EFE8D8; pale mint #9DCAB4; muted lilac #AA9BC9; polished dark joints #495260; amber controller #F2C276.

Color values are palette targets for later material work; image generators may approximate them. Pair important cues with geometry, posture, and contrast so they do not depend on color alone.

## Abilities and movement

Alternates laser sweeps, marked syringe volleys, and capped repair cycles. Interrupting the active care station exposes the chest controller. Later one support robot may remain active during an attack.

Maintains a composed head while the arms move in precise coordinated arcs. A light head draws the laser path before a sweep. Syringe arms align in a visible aiming pose. During repair, the lower clamps fold inward and a link connects to one external station.

## Openings and limitations

Central controller behind the opened circular chest door after a repair interruption. All external active stations must be visible targets from ordinary platforms.

## Parts to keep separate for modeling

Head; central shell; chest door and controller; six arms with explicit shoulder, elbow, and wrist pivots; two light heads; two launchers; two clamps; gimbal. Model three care stations and support robots separately.

Preserve articulation clearance in the neutral pose. Keep projectiles, attack trails, glow cards, impact effects, and environmental props separate from the core model. A part list describes visual assembly, not manufacturing internals.

## Required pose and state references

Neutral balanced arms; laser warning; sweep; syringe aim; volley; repair link; interrupted repair with controller exposed; phase-two support signal; disabled hang.

Make these as separate studies after the neutral design is approved. Use the approved neutral image as a reference so poses do not silently change anatomy or equipment.

## Consistency rules

Exactly six arms in three pairs; no walking legs, organic material, extra face, or robotic-zombie conversion. Keep each arm pair's tool type consistent across all views.

Anatomical left and right refer to the subject's own sides, not the viewer's. Do not automatically mirror an asymmetrical design when generating the opposite view.

## Arena relationship and phase changes

A surgical theater with rising beds and three external care stations. Station links should be readable ribbons of light that do not hide the boss or the platforms.

Phase two introduces one coordinated helper at a time and tighter scheduling. Armor damage may expose service seams, but her body remains the same six-arm surgical robot.

## Before modeling

- Approve one neutral design as the visual master; keep all later images tied to it.
- Compare the same mechanical seams, anatomy, attachment sides, and part counts across every view.
- Check the side silhouette at small gameplay size and in grayscale; important targets and attack poses must remain readable.
- Block out the main forms and test the required poses before adding small surface details. Resolve conflicts between generated views deliberately rather than averaging them blindly.
- Treat the turnaround as concept reference. It is not a guaranteed dimensionally consistent blueprint or a ready-to-use 3D mesh.
