# Staffer

**Direction (C14–C35, 2026-09-29; C56–C60, 2026-10-12):** mature corporate-cyberpunk sci-fi — evil corporation Arcadia Dynamics, sentient AI Adam, rogue AI researcher Dave Harlan; human, cyborg and machine enemies with guns; visible blood; no zombies, no stealth. Enemy art: painted HD parts on a lit cutout rig (C56), in the corporate cyberpunk look (C58), with alert, composed people (C57) and neon trim per controller (C60) ([style guide](../style-guide.md)).

**Asset ID:** LK01\
**Category:** linked (people driven by Adam through the Link)\
**First appearance:** Level 1 (appears in L1–L10; first seen at the Level 1 alarm exit, after the core-node lockdown)\
**Design status:** Look follows C60 (approved 2026-10-12): painted HD parts on the lit cutout rig (C56), corporate cyberpunk (C58), alert and composed (C57), electric-blue trim for the Linked (C60). **Painted sheets are in the game** (three looks, v2, processed 2026-10-12); the roster entry (C31) and human enemies (C25) are confirmed and the lit cutout method is confirmed (C35). Behavior numbers (P23) are *proposed*. Dimensions are provisional art proportions, not engine specifications.

**Look updates:** C57 (2026-10-11): no sleepy or zombie-like look; the Link shows as technology (port light, electric-blue eye glint, synchronized precise moves). C60 (2026-10-12): the port is a clean ring-lit disc with no staples, bruise ring or seam; electric blue #4D8DFF is now the neon color of every Linked type; in Level 2 the Staffers are Dave's old alignment team.

## Identity and role

Basic Linked enemy: a person driven through a broken routine, who drops into a low lunge and grabs. It never blocks progress.

*Proposed identity:* a night-shift Arcadia employee in facilities and office support, who took the Link as a wellness perk. After Dave plugs into the core node in Level 1 and the campus locks down, Adam drives the staff still on shift toward the alarm exit: the Staffer is the first Linked person Dave meets, and the first sign that the calm voice can move people. Adam's order is a routine, not violence. The Staffer wants Dave back at his workstation, and it will hold him there with its hands.

**Three looks on one rig** (C56, C60), all Caucasian (C43): **A, the stocky office worker** (in his forties, close-shaved scalp, a neat short beard); **B, the tall thin colleague** (in his twenties, short tidy hair, glasses); **C, the woman in her fifties** (grey hair in a sharp short bob, reading glasses on a cord). The game picks a look by entity id; stats, tells and timing are the same. **In Level 2** (Alignment, C59) they are Dave's old alignment team, in the same kit with a lab badge on the lanyard.

Staffers appear in Levels 1–10, in the workwear of each act, and they never block progress: Dave can always jump past one. Combat is lethal (C28): a Staffer bleeds red and sparks at the implant (C29), and most Linked deaths are silent, with the port light just going out. Killing one is a choice, since it only reaches for Dave and never bars the route.

## Scale and silhouette

About 1.72 m tall, standing fully upright, roughly 7.25 heads, with an everyday office-worker build (the looks vary it within the rig: A stocky, B tall and thin, C average).

An everyday office-worker body with the head level and held high, square shoulders, arms hanging relaxed a little away from the torso, and two straight legs set under the hips. It reads as an everyday person in a work jacket, standing tidy and precise; only the padded grips strapped over both palms, the blue stripe and the ring-lit port behind the ear mark it as Linked. In grayscale it is the plainest upright human shape in the roster, with a bare head, no headgear and no held item.

Use a neutral 1.70 m adult silhouette as a temporary scale reference on a separate comparison sheet. Dave's final design is not fixed by this measurement.

## Appearance and construction

*Proposed design (C60).* The Act 1 workwear, which is the default skin: a neat navy Arcadia staff jacket with a thin electric-blue stripe (#4D8DFF) down the shoulder and both sleeves, zipped halfway over a pale shirt with an open, squarely turned collar, charcoal trousers and clean slate shoes with pale rubber soles. The near sleeve is pushed up above the elbow, and the shoes are laced tight. A lanyard holds a blank ID card. Both hands wear padded restraint grips: slate rubber pads strapped over the palms with graphite wrist straps, which carry the tell glow in play. The face is alert and composed: wide-open eyes with a steady, attentive gaze, a calm, polite, slightly too-perfect expression with an even, neat mouth, and smooth skin under the eyes.

The Link signature: behind the near ear sits the Link port, a clean coin-sized disc with a pale steel rim and a thin ring light, set flush in the skin. There are no staples, bruise rings, seams or lines under the skin. The hair is close-shaved in look A and short and tidy in looks B and C. The port is painted dark and unlit; when Adam drives the body the engine adds a small, dim, steady amber point there, and when the body dies the light goes dark. The eyes are wide open and alert and are painted without glow; the engine adds a faint electric-blue glint (#4D8DFF) in the irises while Adam drives the body, and it goes dark on death. The port is clean technology, never a monster feature, and nothing is exposed. There are no logos or legible text.

*Workwear per act (skins on the same body):* Act 1, the navy staff jacket above; Act 2, a slate server-hall vest over a grey polo, with a lanyard; Act 3, a pale clinic porter tunic; Act 4, a pale grey Garden-crew smock. Only the jacket, vest, tunic or smock changes; the body, hands, port and grips stay the same.

Pair important cues with geometry, posture and contrast so they do not depend on color alone: the light states differ by size and pattern (a small steady point for driven, a large glow for the tell), and the tell also reads by pose (the crouch) and by the calm bark.

## Abilities and movement

- **Template:** Brawler (low lunge) (C26).
- **Weapon:** none: a restraint grab with both hands.
- **Tell:** GLOW: it drops into a crouch as a large glow on both hands goes amber, then red for the last 0.25 s ("Please return to your workstation, Dave").
- **Attack:** a fast low grab, a low lunge with both hands.
- **Counter:** jump the grab and hit it while it stumbles (2 hits), or jump past it. It never blocks progress.

A brisk, purposeful, even-paced walk, upright, with the head level and the arms swinging evenly. Staffers in a group step and turn on the same beat (flavor only; it changes no timing). Now and then the Staffer stops dead and, in one exact motion, taps its ID card against a door reader that is not there, then walks on at the same pace (flavor only; it changes no timing). The windup is crisp: it stops, the head turns to Dave and locks on, the shoulders square, the fingers spread and the body drops straight into a crouch with the hands out.

The lunge is sudden, fast and fully committed, with the head and torso leading before the feet catch up. A missed grab leaves it stumbling forward, arms out and off balance: that stumble is the opening, the one moment its precision breaks. Its eyes stay on Dave throughout.

**Sample barks** *(proposed; Adam's calm words in the Staffer's own even, polite voice. Each is subtitle text plus a non-verbal shout or grunt sound, since there is no voice pipeline)*:

- "Please return to your workstation, Dave." (the windup)
- "It's very late, Dave." (idle)
- "Have you badged in?" (idle)
- "Good evening, Dr. Harlan." (first sight)

Most deaths are silent: a fall, a spark at the port, and the light going out. Rarely a Staffer says something startled and human, such as "...Dave? Is that you?"

## Openings and limitations

The stumble after a missed grab is the opening, and the readable windup (the head lock, the crouch and the hand glow) gives Dave time to jump. Do not add armor plates, a held weapon or a glowing chest core: the port is a small light behind the ear, not a weak-point target. The Staffer never blocks Dave's route.

## Rig parts, normal maps and sockets

**Looks (C56):** the three looks share one part list, skeleton, pivots, clips and hit zones; the head, hair and build differ. **Parts** *(painted once per look, facing right; near means the side toward the camera)*: head (the port is a small separate head part behind the near ear, with its ring light); torso with the jacket; pelvis; near and far upper arms, lower arms and hands (both hands are grip hands with padded palms; the near hand is the separate weapon-hand part); near and far upper legs, lower legs and feet. Small swinging pieces: the lanyard and blank ID card, and the swapped jacket, vest, tunic or smock for each act's skin. There is no held weapon.

**Pivots:** neck base (the head turns to Dave and locks level), shoulders, elbows, wrists, hip, knees and ankles; the lanyard at the collar. Paint hidden overlap under every joint, and paint the far limbs and the hidden side of the torso complete.

**Sockets:** no gun or weapon socket, since the grab uses the hand parts. A port-light socket at the port holds the small steady amber point (dark on death). Two hand-glow sockets, one on each palm, hold the large tell glow. A spark socket at the port throws the white implant sparks.

**Normal maps:** one per part (green = up), soft-edged: jacket seams and the pushed-up sleeve, collar and zip, lanyard, rubber grip pads and straps, shoe soles, the port rim and ring light, and gentle face modeling with open lids so a lamp lights one side of the face.

**Fluid:** human blood (Blood #B3212F, drying to #8A1A26), drawn by the engine: a spray at the hit point, wound marks attached to the hit part and floor pools. The Linked also throw white implant sparks at the port. Blood anchors sit on the head, chest, belly, near upper arm and near thigh.

**Motion and death:** hand-keyed on the rig for the side view (C38): a brisk, even walk with a steady arm swing, a low crouch-and-lunge grab, a stumble recovery and a hit flinch, plus the relaxed arm hang, the head-lock turn and the crouch. Death is a ragdoll pushed by the killing shot, and the port light goes dark. The body then stays as a static corpse, restored after a death or Continue.

Plan the blood, the tell glows, the muzzle flashes, the implant lights and every other effect as separate layers, never painted into the parts. These are the intended rig components, not a finished rig: the lit Night Guard test settles the tool, the pivots and the clip retargeting.

## Required pose and state references

Neutral upright stance; walk (brisk, even stride); routine (ID-card tap, flavor only); windup (head lock, then the low crouch with the hands out); attack (the low grab lunge); stumble recovery (the opening); hit reaction; death launch (first ragdoll frame); resting corpse pose; the grab hands (open with padded palms out, closed grip).

Tell by pose: the head lock and the low crouch with the hands out are the windup, and the engine adds the large amber-then-red glow on both hands over that pose. The small steady amber point at the port is separate: it is present through every driven pose and goes dark on death. The resting corpse pose is the one authored pose restored after a death or Continue: crumpled where it fell, ID card askew.

Make these as separate studies after the neutral design is approved. Use the approved neutral image as a reference so poses do not silently change anatomy or equipment.

## Consistency rules

Realistic adult proportions (about 7.25 heads), two arms and two legs, one port, two padded grips. No held weapon, no armor plates, no full robotic body, no exposed wiring, organs or wounds, no staples, stitches, bruise rings or seams, no readable text or logos, no violet or purple. Eyes wide open and alert, painted without glow (the engine adds the faint electric-blue iris glint); no sleepy, slack or vacant faces. No blood, wound, glow or shadow painted into the parts. No dismemberment. The implant is clean technology, never drawn as monstrous in itself.

**Mirroring** *(proposed)*: paint once, facing right, and mirror the rig for left-facing play. The pushed-up sleeve, the near shoe and the lanyard sit on the near side and are safe to mirror. The port is a small separate head part, a round disc with no handedness, so it sits behind the near ear in either facing. The workwear skins swap only the jacket part.
