# Patchbot

**Asset ID:** R07\
**Category:** robots\
**First appearance:** Level 4; a separately labeled converted variant appears in level 10\
**Design status:** Proposed visual direction for the established concept. Dimensions are provisional art proportions, not engine specifications.

## Identity and role

Small repair-support robot that should be easy to recognize among larger enemies.

## Scale and silhouette

0.70 m tall and 0.75 m wide with side arms tucked.

A squat toolbox body on three wheels, with a hinged upper lid, a tiny periscope eye, and two folding tool arms mounted one on each side.

Use a neutral 1.70 m human silhouette as a temporary scale reference on a separate comparison sheet. The hero's final design is not fixed by this measurement.

## Appearance and construction

The front panel has a cheerful two-slot speaker and a small leaf service badge. The left arm ends in a round repair pad; the right in a three-prong gripper. The lid opens to show neat replaceable tool trays. A copper cable spool sits low on the back. Make hinges, handle, and latch oversized.

## Color and materials

Service orange #E99B4C; cream #EFE2C9; teal #418C8A; dark rubber #354044; soft cyan repair light #77DAD7.

Color values are palette targets for later material work; image generators may approximate them. Pair important cues with geometry, posture, and contrast so they do not depend on color alone.

## Abilities and movement

Repairs damaged robots and reactivates fallen machines. It supports encounters rather than dealing heavy direct damage.

Scoots quickly between machines, crouches on its suspension to repair, and stretches both tool arms toward the target. It folds its tools and flees when approached.

## Openings and limitations

Its exposed compact body is vulnerable while repairing. The open tool cavity provides an obvious firing window, without adding a new mandatory weak-point mechanic.

## Parts to keep separate for modeling

Toolbox shell; hinged lid; two segmented tool arms; eye stalk; three wheels; rear cable spool; replaceable internal tray.

Preserve articulation clearance in the neutral pose. Keep projectiles, attack trails, glow cards, impact effects, and environmental props separate from the core model. A part list describes visual assembly, not manufacturing internals.

## Required pose and state references

Closed patrol; lid open repair; arms extended; flee; disabled. Separate late-story sheet: converted lid-open state with neural graft material.

Make these as separate studies after the neutral design is approved. Use the approved neutral image as a reference so poses do not silently change anatomy or equipment.

## Consistency rules

Base Patchbot is fully mechanical. Exactly two tool arms and three wheels. Never mix the late-story organic conversion into early-level references.

Anatomical left and right refer to the subject's own sides, not the viewer's. Do not automatically mirror an asymmetrical design when generating the opposite view.

## Separate late-story variant

Level 10 converted Patchbot: retain the same shell, wheels, eye, and outer silhouette. Replace only the internal tray with an installed neural-interface cradle containing one folded coral tissue ribbon and two thick vein-like tendrils. When the lid opens, the ribbon unfolds toward another robot's matching neural port. It transfers tissue by contact, never by a wireless infection beam. Use a separate image prompt and separate internal mesh variant; base robots remain immune unless upgraded.

## Before modeling

- Approve one neutral design as the visual master; keep all later images tied to it.
- Compare the same mechanical seams, anatomy, attachment sides, and part counts across every view.
- Check the side silhouette at small gameplay size and in grayscale; important targets and attack poses must remain readable.
- Block out the main forms and test the required poses before adding small surface details. Resolve conflicts between generated views deliberately rather than averaging them blindly.
- Treat the turnaround as concept reference. It is not a guaranteed dimensionally consistent blueprint or a ready-to-use 3D mesh.
