# Courtesy Officer

**Approved visual direction (C11):** [Hand-drawn 2D](../style-guide.md) — clean outlines, flat colors, cel shadows and layered scenery. [Selected references](../../concept-art/README.md).

**Asset ID:** R02\
**Category:** robots\
**First appearance:** Level 3\
**Design status:** Confirmed hand-drawn 2D rendering style (C11); this asset's unselected appearance details remain proposed. Dimensions are provisional art proportions, not engine specifications.

## Identity and role

Shield defender; a neighborhood security machine issuing aggressively polite warnings.

## Scale and silhouette

1.85 m tall; broad shield approximately 1.15 m tall.

A squat humanoid with a rounded police-box torso, small domed head, two arms, and two short sturdy legs. A large rounded rectangular shield occupies the anatomical left arm; a compact baton is held in the right.

Use a neutral 1.70 m human silhouette as a temporary scale reference on a separate comparison sheet. The hero's final design is not fixed by this measurement.

## Appearance and construction

Use a visor with two cyan horizontal eyes and a tiny speaker grille shaped like a fixed smile. The chest has an embossed leaf-in-circle service emblem. The shield is a solid cream plate with a mint rim, one viewing slit, and a lower beveled corner. The back carries a protruding vented battery pack. Feet are wide rubber-soled wedges.

## Color and materials

Deep teal #22575B; cream #EDE3CB; dark steel #3B474B; soft cyan #6BE7DF. Keep scuffs on the shield perimeter and boot fronts.

Color values are palette targets for later material work; image generators may approximate them. Pair important cues with geometry, posture, and contrast so they do not depend on color alone.

## Abilities and movement

Blocks frontal shots with its shield and attacks nearby targets with a stun baton. Its authority gestures create flanking opportunities.

Walks in measured heel-to-toe steps with the shield forward. Raises the baton and flashes a warning lamp before a swing. While delivering a long announcement, the torso swivels theatrically and briefly exposes the back.

## Openings and limitations

Rear battery grille; show a clear mechanical opening with three broad horizontal slots. The shield remains a meaningful obstruction, not a translucent weak spot.

## Parts to keep separate for animation

Head; torso; two articulated arms; shield; baton; two legs; rear battery; separate shoulder and knee covers. Shield mounts must stay consistent across views.

Plan overlapping drawing layers and visible pivots for the intended animation method. Keep projectiles, attack trails, warning overlays, impacts and environmental props separate from the character or weapon. These are illustrated components, not a mandated rig: frame-by-frame, cutout or hybrid animation remains a later production choice.

## Required pose and state references

Guard stance; warning gesture; baton wind-up; baton strike; rear exposure; stunned; disabled.

Make these as separate studies after the neutral design is approved. Use the approved neutral image as a reference so poses do not silently change anatomy or equipment.

## Consistency rules

Shield always on anatomical left, baton always right. No firearm, cape, human face, organic parts, or mirrored equipment changes between views.

Anatomical left and right refer to the subject's own sides, not the viewer's. Do not automatically mirror an asymmetrical design when generating the opposite view.
