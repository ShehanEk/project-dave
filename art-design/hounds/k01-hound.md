# Hound

**Direction (C14–C35, 2026-09-29; C56–C60, 2026-10-12):** mature corporate-cyberpunk sci-fi — evil corporation Arcadia Dynamics, sentient AI Adam, rogue AI researcher Dave Harlan; human, cyborg and machine enemies with guns; visible blood; no zombies, no stealth. Enemy art: painted HD parts on a lit cutout rig (C56), in the corporate cyberpunk look (C58), with alert, composed people (C57) and neon trim per controller (C60) ([style guide](../style-guide.md)).

**Asset ID:** K01\
**Category:** hounds\
**First appearance:** Level 2 (Alignment, C59), with a Night Guard handler through Level 3; Adam-driven in Levels 7–8; Garden-built in Level 11\
**Design status:** Roster entry confirmed (C30, C31). Look follows C60 (approved 2026-10-12): painted HD parts on the lit cutout rig (C56), corporate cyberpunk (C58), neon per controller (lime with a handler, magenta when Adam drives it). Concept images are still to be generated; Level 2's parts sheets (the Hound's looks) start with this type. Dimensions are provisional art proportions, not engine specifications.

## Identity and role

Cyborg guard dog and the first fast melee threat: it drops low, lunges, lunges again, then stands rigid.

**Who built and fields it *(proposed)*:** Arcadia Security runs a K9 unit on the Eon City campus. The dogs are real working dogs, shepherd-type, fitted with Arcadia's K9 kit: a slim smoked visor band over the eyes, a steel jaw guard, white composite plates on the shoulder and spine, and a collar control module. Night Guards ([SE01](../security/se01-night-guard.md)) handle them in Levels 2 and 3 (Act 1): in Level 2 (Alignment, C59) the Hound waits in the Cognition Lab's K9 kennel with a Night Guard handler, and a second pair holds the freight-lift approach. In Levels 7 and 8 Adam drives the same dogs directly through their collar modules, with no handler. In Level 11 it builds its own copies in the Garden.

**Why it attacks Dave:** in Act 1 the handler sets it on Dave, who has been flagged as a security threat. Later Adam simply sends it. It is an animal doing what it was trained to do, which is what makes it unsettling rather than cute. The hardware is shown as a clean, expensive working kit, not as horror.

## Scale and silhouette

0.70 m at the shoulder plates; about 1.30 m from nose to rump, plus a 0.40 m tail. In the lunge the body stretches to about 1.70 m.

A lean, deep-chested shepherd-type dog held low and forward: a long muzzle with a steel jaw guard that sits slightly forward, a slim visor band across the eyes, two upright ears, a ridge of overlapping composite plates along the spine with a thin neon strip, a plated shoulder guard, and a natural tail carried low. The jaw guard, the spine ridge and the visor band make it read as a sleek armored working dog at gameplay size.

Use a neutral 1.70 m human silhouette as a temporary scale reference on a separate comparison sheet. Dave's final design is not fixed by this measurement.

## Appearance and construction

*Proposed design (C60).* A real dog first, with the K9 kit fitted cleanly, like a premium security product.

- **Body:** shepherd-type build with a short, dense, well-groomed coat: black saddle over the back, tan on the legs, muzzle and chest. Real paws with dark pads and visible claws; no boots.
- **Head:** a natural upper muzzle and skull with a dark nose and alert dark eyes behind a slim smoked-glass visor band (its own part) on a strap round the head, with a small round sensor at its centre. The real lower jaw is covered by a brushed-steel jaw guard, a slim plate along the jaw and chin with a thin slot light along the teeth line (painted dark; the tell glows there) and a round servo capsule at the hinge. It is held shut at rest: no tongue and no panting. Both ears stand upright.
- **Throat and collar:** a wide gunmetal collar band carries the K9 control module at the throat. A small status light sits on each side of the band, so the state reads from both facings.
- **Harness and plates:** a fitted navy tactical harness (Arcadia Security's, [SE01](../security/se01-night-guard.md)) with a rigid grab handle across the shoulders, a pale reflective band and a small white Arcadia emblem patch on the near shoulder (an abstract arch-and-leaf mark, no lettering; the final logo is authored separately). White composite plates guard the shoulder and run along the spine as a ridge of five linked plates, bolted through the harness at clean seams. A thin lime neon strip (#C6FF3D, Arcadia Security's color) runs along the spine plates, painted flat with no glow. No leash is drawn, only a short cut-off leash ring on the handle.
- **Tail:** a natural tail, carried low and straight, with no hardware on it.

**Three looks on one rig** (C56, C60): the dog shares one part list, skeleton, clips and hit zones in every look. **A, the Arcadia K9** (Levels 2–3): as above, with a lime spine strip. **B, Adam-driven** (Levels 7–8): the same body and plates repainted. The handle and leash ring are removed, the harness and plates are graphite, the spine strip is magenta (#FF3DD5, Adam's color), and the collar lights are small dim amber. The coat stays clean. **C, Garden-built** (Level 11): Adam's copy on the same skeleton, an unpainted factory-white polymer shell with assembly markings and thin magenta seams, no fur and no harness, with a smooth sensor visor band and a polymer jaw guard, and the same plates, jaw and tail shape. It is a machine, and like every Garden-built variant it shortens only the amber part of the tell; red stays 0.25 s.

## Color and materials

*Proposed palette (Arcadia K9):* coat black #24211F and tan #A9784A; gunmetal #5A6B80 for the jaw guard and collar; smoked visor glass #14181E; plate white composite #E2E7EC; harness navy #2A3B5C with a pale band #D3DAE0 and an emblem patch white #E9EDF0; neon lime strip #C6FF3D. *Adam-driven look:* harness and plates graphite #3A4350, spine strip magenta #FF3DD5. *Garden-built look:* factory-white polymer #E8ECEE, assembly marks #2E3B4E, magenta seams #FF3DD5.

Everything is painted as evenly lit HD parts (C56): soft painted shading for form, clean dark outlines, no cast shadow, rim light or glow. The engine's lamps light the coat, plates and jaw guard smoothly through their normal maps. Keep the tan coat and the plates in mid-to-light values so the dog holds its silhouette when the scenery is dim; the black saddle is the only large dark area. Paint the far-side legs and ear a few percent darker as a flat local color, not as a shadow.

**Light and fluid *(proposed)*:**

- While the dog hunts, the visor band's centre sensor shows only a small, dim, steady amber point (#FFB02E), as on every driven or hunting body. Otherwise it is dark glass.
- Collar lights (a repeater on each side, so the state reads from both facings): steady teal #3FE0D0 while the dog is idle or waiting under a handler, and the same small, dim, steady amber point once it hunts. Neither is a large glow, and neither is a detection or alert state (C16).
- The tell is a large additive glow on the jaw guard, along the teeth line and the hinge capsule: amber, then alarm red #FF3B4E for the last 0.25 s, on every lunge.
- Blood is red #B3212F, drying to #8A1A26, drawn as a separate spray, wound marks and floor pools, never painted into the base art. The plates throw white sparks instead. Blood never glows, never uses a tell color and never covers a tell, a ledge or a pickup.
- The Garden-built look does not bleed red. It throws white sparks and leaks black oil #14181E (with its sheen rim) like every machine, and its sensor band carries the same small amber point when it hunts.
- **Neon (C60):** the spine strip is the neon and marks who controls the dog: lime #C6FF3D with a handler (Arcadia Security), magenta #FF3DD5 when Adam drives it or builds it. It is steady, thinner and dimmer than any tell, never on the jaw guard, and it goes dark on death.

Color values are palette targets for later material work; image generators may approximate them. Pair important cues with geometry, posture, and contrast so they do not depend on color alone.

## Abilities and movement

**Template:** Charger (light, with a dash counter) (C26). **Weapon:** steel jaw bite (melee). Distances are in H, Dave's height, the gameplay unit. The numbers are proposed (P23) and untested.

- **Tell (GLOW):** it drops low, chest near the floor and haunches up, as the jaw guard glows amber and then red (red for the last 0.25 s), with a low rising growl and a servo whine.
- **Attack:** a lunge of 2 H along the floor. After a 0.5 s crouch it lunges again with its own amber-then-red blip: 2 lunges in Act 1, 3 in Levels 7–8 and 11. At least 0.9 s pass from one lunge to the next. In Act 1 the handler's own wind-up starts 1.5 s after the chain ends, so dog and handler never attack together.
- **Counter:** jump each lunge, then shoot it during its rigid recovery. Aim slightly down. 2 hits.

It trots low, head below the shoulders, jaw shut and tail carried low. It never pants or wags. In recovery it skids to a stop with the legs braced stiff, the head up and the jaw-guard glow gone.

**Death:** it bleeds red and sparks at its plates, its legs twitch for about 1 s, and the body then stays as a static corpse.

## Openings and limitations

The rigid recovery after each lunge chain: a braced, stiff stop with the jaw dark. There is no armored weak point and no invulnerable state. The plates protect the shoulder and spine, but a hit anywhere counts. Keep the whole body one low, readable target.

## Rig parts, normal maps and sockets

- **Parts (four-legged rig, rigid parts):** head (skull and upper muzzle, with both ears attached); visor band (its own part, over both eyes); lower jaw with the steel jaw guard; neck; collar module with a separate light layer on each side; chest; rump; harness and handle (an overlay part, so the skins can swap it); shoulder plate; five spine plates; four legs, each in two parts (upper leg; lower leg with paw, the hind legs with a clear hock); tail in two segments.
- **Pivots:** neck base; jaw hinge at the servo capsule; the top of each upper leg; the knee or elbow and the hock; the tail root at the rump; the rump as the root part.
- **Normal maps:** one per part, green = up: fine fur-flow relief on the coat, crisp bevels and bolt heads on the plates, brushed bevels on the jaw guard, the curved glass of the visor band, fur on the tail.
- **Sockets:** no gun socket. Light sockets at the two collar lights, the visor sensor and the jaw guard (the additive tell glow, attached to the lower jaw part). Spark points at each plate.
- **Fluid:** red blood: a spray at the hit point along the shot direction, wound marks attached to the hit part and a floor pool that glints under lamps. The Garden-built look leaks black oil and throws sparks instead. All are separate from the painted art.
- **Motion and death:** the dog's trot, crouch, lunge and stiff stop are hand-keyed on the rigid parts, like every enemy (C38). On death the parts become physics bodies pushed by the killing shot (a ragdoll), still joined at their pivots: no dismemberment (C29). The four leg parts twitch for about 1 s (a two-frame swap), then the body settles as a static corpse with a blood overlay.

Plan overlapping drawing layers and visible pivots for the rig. Keep blood, sparks, oil, glows, muzzle flashes, projectiles, attack trails, warning overlays, impacts and environmental props separate from the character. The cutout method is confirmed direction, validated by the approved lit-cutout test (2026-09-30) (C35); frame counts, timing and export remain later production choices.

## Required pose and state references

Standing side view (neutral, jaw shut, legs apart so the near and far legs separate); stalking trot; tell crouch (low, jaw glow amber, then red); lunge (body stretched, jaw open); rigid recovery (braced stop); hit reaction; death frame (on its side, legs out) and the two-frame leg twitch. Looks: Arcadia K9 (lime spine strip, teal collar lights while idle), Adam-driven (no handle, magenta strip, small amber collar lights) and Garden-built (factory-white polymer, magenta seams).

Show lamp and jaw states as flat colored shapes with no glow halo: amber in the first half of the tell crouch, red in the last quarter, dark in recovery. Glows, sparks and blood are added later as separate layers.

Make these as separate studies after the neutral design is approved. Use the approved neutral image as a reference so poses do not silently change anatomy or equipment.

## Consistency rules

Four legs, one steel jaw guard, one visor band over both eyes and a natural tail, one shoulder plate and five spine plates. No tongue, no panting, no barking pose, no wagging, no floppy ears and no cute proportions. No leash drawn, no human features, no exposed organs or bones, no staples, sutures, matted fur or empty eye sockets, and no dismemberment. Plates never cover the jaw hinge or the visor. Blood, sparks and glows are never painted into the base art, and fluid never uses the tell colors. The collar lights are readability states only, not a detection or alert meter (C16).

**Mirroring** *(proposed)*: paint once, facing right, and mirror the rig for left-facing play. Anatomical left and right belong to the character, so an asymmetric part must be mirror-safe or sit on a separate part the rig can swap. The visor band is its own part and spans both eyes, so it mirrors cleanly, and the collar lights repeat the state on both sides. Everything else is symmetric or centered, so the dog mirrors safely.
