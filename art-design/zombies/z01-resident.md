## Identity and role

Basic shambler; a former neighbor caught in a broken daily routine.

## Scale and silhouette

1.65 m upright, approximately 1.45 m in its habitual slouch.

A soft pear-shaped human body with a small forward head, drooping shoulders, two ordinary arms, and two unevenly bent legs. The silhouette must remain recognizable as an everyday person.

Use a neutral 1.70 m human silhouette as a temporary scale reference on a separate comparison sheet. The hero's final design is not fixed by this measurement.

## Appearance and construction

Choose one fixed base outfit: faded coral cardigan over a cream shirt, teal trousers, and broad house slippers. One cardigan sleeve is rolled higher than the other. The face has heavy eyelids, an unfocused gaze, a slack lower lip, and slightly sunken cheeks. Closed medical seam lines at the neck and wrist suggest repeated treatment without exposed organs.

## Color and materials

Desaturated sage skin #9AAA88; faded coral #C98979; cream #DCD1B9; dusty teal #658F8A; muted plum shadow #766E83. Cloth is matte, skin softly waxy.

Color values are palette targets for later material work; image generators may approximate them. Pair important cues with geometry, posture, and contrast so they do not depend on color alone.

## Abilities and movement

Slow approach with occasional short lunges. Threat increases in groups rather than through complex individual attacks.

Shuffles with one dragging slipper, occasionally repeats a small wave, then stumbles into a clearly prepared forward lunge. The head and torso lead before the feet catch up.

## Openings and limitations

Ordinary body hits are effective; the long lunge preparation and recovery are the main openings. Do not add a glowing artificial core.

## Parts to keep separate for animation

One humanoid body with two arms and two legs; separate cardigan, shirt collar, trousers, and slippers where useful; simple facial controls for lids and jaw.

Plan overlapping drawing layers and visible pivots for the intended animation method. Keep projectiles, attack trails, warning overlays, impacts and environmental props separate from the character or weapon. These are illustrated components, not a mandated rig: frame-by-frame, cutout or hybrid animation remains a later production choice.

## Required pose and state references

Neutral slouch; harmless habitual wave; lunge preparation; lunge; stumble recovery; hit reaction; defeated rest.

Make these as separate studies after the neutral design is approved. Use the approved neutral image as a reference so poses do not silently change anatomy or equipment.

## Consistency rules

Two arms, two legs, no weapon, no robotic body, no exposed gore. Keep the same cardigan, rolled sleeve, and slippers in every view.

Anatomical left and right refer to the subject's own sides, not the viewer's. Do not automatically mirror an asymmetrical design when generating the opposite view.
